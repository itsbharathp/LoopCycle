PART ONE · THE TECHNOLOGY

What is a large language model?

A prediction engine that has read more text than any person ever could.

It writes one word at a time, always choosing a likely next word. That's why it sounds fluent — and why fluent is not the same as true.

PART ONE · USING IT WELL

Where it shines, where it stumbles

Shines

  • First drafts in seconds
  • Ten headline options instead of one
  • Rewriting one story for many audiences
  • Translating and localising across languages
  • Turning long reports into short posts

Stumbles

  • Inventing facts, figures and quotes
  • A generic, over-polished "AI voice"
  • Not knowing your context or community
  • Missing local and cultural nuance
  • Not knowing recent events

Use it for speed and range. Bring the facts, the context and the voice yourself.

PART TWO · PROMPTING

Five parts of a good brief

Example: an Instagram caption for a rice brand working with smallholder farmers

Role

"You are the social media writer for [Brand], an impact brand."

Task

"Write an Instagram caption announcing our new harvest."

Context

"We buy organic rice directly from smallholder farmers in [region]. Our followers are young urban families who care where their food comes from."

Format

"Under 80 words. Warm, not preachy. End with a question. Three hashtags."

Examples

"Here are two past posts that sound like us: [paste posts]"

PART TWO · PROMPTING

Same request, two briefs

Vague

"Write a post about our recycling."

You get: generic copy that could be any brand, possibly with an invented statistic.

Specific

"You are the comms lead at [Brand], which turns discarded fishing nets into [product] in [country]. Write a LinkedIn post for corporate buyers about our new collection. Confident, not preachy. About 120 words. Use only these facts: [facts]. End with a clear call to action."

You get: on-brand, factual copy that's nearly ready to publish.

PART TWO · PROMPTING

Five moves that make the difference

1

Show your voice

"Here are three of our past posts. Match this tone."
2

Ask for options

"Give me ten headlines, from playful to serious."
3

Let it interview you

"Before you write, ask me three questions."
4

Edit like an editor

"Shorter. Warmer. Cut the jargon."
5

Ask for a critique

"What would a sceptical buyer question in this post?"

The first answer is a first draft. Keep the conversation going.

PART TWO · SAFETY

Four guardrails for impact brands

Check every number

Your impact claims are your credibility. An invented statistic is greenwashing, even by accident.

Protect people

Keep beneficiary names, photos and donor data out of public AI tools.

Keep your voice

Edit until it sounds like you. Your community will notice if it doesn't.

Be honest with images

Never present AI-made images of people, communities or places as real.

ANALYSE · Q1 ANSWER KEY

What worked? Facilitator findings

  • Reels: avg reach ~16.5K (median 10.7K). Carousels 4.9K. Images 2.0K.
  • Engagement rate: Reels 8.9%, carousels 8.8%, images 5.8% — formats are close on engagement.
  • Carousels win on save rate (3.5% vs 1.3% for Reels). Reels win on share rate (2.4% vs 0.7%).
  • By pillar: Loop Tote best engagement (8.7%) and save rate (2.9%). Picker stories best share rate (1.8%).
  • Behind the scenes weakest overall (6.8% engagement).
  • TRAP: Education looks top for reach (28.2K avg) but has only 3 posts. One Reel hit 76K. Without it avg ≈ 4.2K.
  • B2B has only 2 posts — no conclusions possible.

Teaching point

Averages mislead on small samples. Always ask the AI to check for outliers before drawing conclusions.

ANALYSE · Q2 ANSWER KEY

Why did our best posts break out? Facilitator findings

  • performance_flag is relative to format median reach (~2× = breakout, ~0.6× = underperformer).
  • Breakouts (3): "bottle journey" Reel Tue 08:00 76K; "1 tonne recycled PET" carousel Fri 20:00 10.6K; river clean-up image Wed 20:00 4.1K.
  • Underperformers (2): Pak Asep story Reel Sat 09:00 4.4K; "fair-trade payment" carousel Wed 20:00 2.4K.
  • Possible pattern: explainers + concrete numbers + community action outperformed personal stories. Only 5 posts — hypothesis only.
  • Hashtag count: 2 for 28 of 30 posts — cannot be tested.
  • Caption length: ~0 correlation with engagement, ~0.23 with reach — barely useful.

Teaching point

Good analysis tells you what it cannot conclude. "Hashtags don't vary — can't test" is more honest than "hashtags don't matter".

ANALYSE · Q3 ANSWER KEY

When should we post? Facilitator findings

  • 08:00 WIB looks best (avg reach ~20.8K vs 3–5K for lunchtime/evening). Tuesday looks best (23.6K avg).
  • THE CATCH: nearly all Reels were at 08:00–09:00; most carousels/images at 12:00–20:00. Time is confounded with format.
  • One viral Reel inflates the 08:00 and Tuesday averages.
  • Among Reels only: median reach 10.4K at 08:00 vs 10.8K at 09:00 — no real difference.
  • Engagement rate barely changes by weekday (~7.7% to 8.8%).
  • modeled_peak_view_hour_wib mostly mirrors posting hour — says little about when audience is active.
  • A good experiment posts the same format at two different times over several weeks.

Teaching point

Confounding variables. Always ask the AI to check whether time mixes with format before claiming a "best time".

ANALYSE · Q5 ANSWER KEY

What should we post next month? What good looks like

  • Mixes Reels (reach, new audiences) with carousels (saves, purchase intent).
  • Uses Loop Tote content for purchase goal; picker stories for trust-building.
  • Links every choice to a real Q1–Q4 finding; marks the rest as "test".

ANALYSE · WRAP-UP

What makes these prompts work

Six ingredients of a useful data prompt

  • Business question first"Which formats should we invest more in?"
  • ContextWho you are, what you sell, what Instagram does for you
  • Data describedWhat each column means, what is blank and why
  • Specific stepsNumbered tasks with named columns
  • Checks built inSample sizes, outliers, confounds, what cannot be concluded
  • A decision at the endRecommendation, content plan, or experiment design

ANALYSE · WRAP-UP

Keeping yourself honest

  • 1 Spot-check a numberPick one figure and recompute it in Excel or Sheets.
  • 2 Ask for the working"Show the calculation and the posts you used."
  • 3 Ask for the opposite view"What would someone who disagrees with this recommendation say?"
  • 4 Watch for confident language on small samplesWords like "proves" or "clearly shows" on fewer than 5 posts are red flags.
